# Product decision

**Recommend an evidence-first editing rehearsal for indie founders' short product demos.** A founder has a launch cut but cannot tell whether its explanation lands. Friends offer vague feedback; posting tests costs a launch opportunity; a score alone does not identify an edit. These pain/workaround claims are hypotheses from the brief, not user research results.

Promise: **“See where your simulated audience loses interest—and the edit worth trying next.”** Avoid “know whether it will go viral.” The practical value is a usable edit; the judge-visible novelty is a scene-by-scene cast that stops watching, with traceable reasons and a paired rerun.

## Alternatives and strategy

| Approach | Strength | Tradeoff | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Score-and-suggest wrapper around a full video | Fast first demo, simplest implementation | Weak differentiation; cannot prove sequential bail-out | Reject as core product; a baseline in validation only |
| Sequential synthetic audience + timestamped editing notes | Inspectable mechanism and a clear edit/retest loop | More inference, correlation/bias, difficult latency | **Choose**, with explicit simulation-only claims |
| Human creative-testing panel | Real respondent opinions; stronger grounding | Recruitment and turnaround; different operating model | Use as a later validation comparator, not runtime dependency |

OpusClip markets moment scoring and clip selection; its public claims establish a relevant alternative, not independently verified effectiveness. PickFu offers recorded human feedback. Frame.io organizes timestamped review. Descript connects text to video edits. Therefore the brief's claim that alternatives never suggest changes is unsupported. Our differentiation hypothesis is the combination of **inspectable stopping decisions + evidence-linked fixes + controlled A/B**, not invention of video feedback. See [dated primary-source research](RESEARCH.md).

## Core journey and proof moments

Open → upload → default Reels/tech audience → watch actual completed reaction events → read headline → select a note and seek its clip → inspect simulated reasons → edit externally → upload B → compare with the same cast/settings → choose a thumbnail → delete both videos.

Three proof moments: (1) a persona skips and no later input is dispatched to it; (2) one note highlights the exact video moment and opens its evidence; (3) A/B shows changed retention and unresolved uncertainty without promising B wins. The graph is a view of events, not evidence of a social network spreading the video.

## Scope

- **MUST, first demo:** validated private upload; anonymous owner session; scene-local features; 50-persona Flash; durable progress/reconnect; top three supported notes; Reels scenario ranges and assumptions; paired A/B; Thumbnail Lab with simulated votes (Muse Image where permitted/API-verified, owned-frame layouts as fallback); model/data provenance; complete deletion path; honest sample replay.
- **SHOULD, after loop works:** hook-only partial what-if; private history polish; watermarked PDF; explicit opt-in expiring share links; optional SAM enrichment and Wonder only after measured value. They remain planned, not silently discarded.
- **LATER, in supplied order:** Deep 300–500 personas; TikTok/Shorts; niche benchmarking; accounts/credits/API; opt-in actuals and accuracy dashboard when real outcome data and a changed accuracy mandate exist. Do not publish benchmark percentiles before a lawful comparable dataset exists.
- **EXPLICIT NON-GOALS:** posting/scheduling, in-app video editing, platform-algorithm replication, guaranteed virality, real-world accuracy validation in this pass, enterprise compliance certification, long-form video.

## Success and stop/continue

Primary hackathon metric: **four of five target founders can identify one supported edit and use A/B to explain what changed within five minutes, without facilitator hints**. Count every recruited attempt; collect time, selected note and their explanation. No sessions have run.

Guardrails: ≥80% valid uploads finish; zero exposed private videos; 100% displayed derived metrics have method/evidence; zero post-skip future inputs; first finding/first verdict/report timing per brief; measured cost within authorized cap. Five-session results are directional, not population estimates.

Adoption experiment: owner invites five founders with owned 20–60s demos; compare their normal feedback workflow to one Viralist-assisted edit, then ask whether they would use it for their next launch. Continue if ≥4 finish and ≥3 choose a specific edit they can justify from evidence. Revise the note/evidence loop if ≤2 do; stop adding features until the reason is understood. Do not use a higher simulated score as proof of product value.

Paid-product hypothesis later: founders will pay to save an editing round. Validate by offering an explicitly described paid concierge pilot at an owner-chosen price after the hackathon; record acceptance/payment rather than asking only “would you pay?”. Pricing and demand are UNKNOWN. No billing implementation or outreach is authorized now.
