# Behavioral differentiation benchmark v1 — frozen before inference

Purpose: determine whether six explicit decision policies produce stable, stimulus-sensitive synthetic judgments. Distinct JSON rows and expressive reasons alone are not successes. This is not a human-audience or reach-validation study.

Seven definitions run: six candidate policies and a generic baseline. An eighth instance duplicates the baseline card under a different opaque ID on every clip; its disagreement estimates label/random-response noise, and never counts as another policy. No protected demographic stereotypes or invented human-fidelity scores.

Media: 30 constructed eight-second muted clips, with identical style and 0–3s/3–8s boundaries. Fourteen pairs cover seven factors (proof, language, payoff order, disclosed processing location, action count, undo behavior, interaction mode). Every pair changes one configuration field. Seven development pairs depict text cleanup; seven heldout pairs depict row deduplication. Two further controls permit consensus. These are static-card toy-operation stimuli, not shipping-product footage or a test of motion realism. Native clips are encoded at 24fps from fixed HyperFrames card states; 1fps source capture loses no transitions because every cut occurs at an integer second.

Two repeats per development cell, three per heldout/control cell: 608 viewer trials, at most 1216 sequential scene decisions. Actual requests can be fewer because a skipped viewer receives no later segment. Randomized cohort order is fixed by seed. Each viewer call is isolated; same policy, scene and prior impression only. There is no pairwise winner prompt. The analysis-only plan, phase labels, reference policy and expected effect are absent from inference payloads. Ground-truth observations are verbatim rendered text, not evaluative tags such as strong proof or low friction. Real current frames are also supplied.

Freeze policy, stimulus, analysis and protocol hashes before the first model run. Use Spark 1.3 Standard. Do not alter policies after inspecting heldout outputs. Development may reveal that the hypothesis fails; there is no automatic prompt tuning or forced-disagreement quota. Current 50-instance baseline is not replaced by these candidates automatically.

Primary diagnostic: repeated ordinal interest changes for the edited variant relative to the preregistered reference policy. A larger response must occur in both development repeats and at least two of three heldout repeats, on the same anchor factor. Report all vectors, zeros, opposite effects and missing data; do not call this statistical significance. Watching through the last segment and willingness to try are separate secondary outcomes. A final stop after seeing the last segment is not early dropout.

A stable per-clip decision signature needs two matching repeats (unanimity when there are only two). Pairwise distinction needs stable differences across development and heldout, on at least max(2, baseline-alias stable-disagreement count + 2) clips. Choose the largest fully separated subset of anchor-supported candidates; deterministic lexical tie-break. This descriptive, noise-referenced count is not an effective statistical sample size. Different wording, row names or roles cannot establish it. Exact stable duplicates may be provisionally aliased; uncertainty is not proof of equivalence.

Keep only the demonstrated prototype subset in `retained-policies.json`; others remain unproven or pairwise unresolved. Missing trials never earn support. Include all diagnostic target/reference reactions and evidence IDs in the matrix; manually inspect representative and contradictory evidence. No claim of 50 distinct behaviors follows from testing six candidates.

Carry prior spend and its unknown-charge reserve forward; use a $9.50 dispatch ceiling below the owner's $10 total. No automatic charged retries. Inspect actual model failures before any bounded recovery. Credentials use private stdin/environment; raw replies and rendered media stay in the private run directory. No publication, GitHub writes, messages or releases.
